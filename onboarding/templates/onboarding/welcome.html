How to read a forecast

Every site gets a recommendation for each day, from three:

GO

Conditions stay inside the limits you set. Nothing to think about.

CAUTION

Something crosses a caution limit at some point in the day. Worth looking at the hourly breakdown before committing a team — often it is a two-hour window rather than the day.

CANCEL

A cancel limit is crossed. We also show how likely that is, and which variable is driving it.

The limits behind those words are yours, not ours — you set them in a moment, and they are what makes the recommendation mean anything.

Where the numbers come from

Each forecast blends four independent weather models — the Met Office UKV, ECMWF, ICON-EU and ARPEGE — rather than trusting one. Where they agree, confidence is high. Where they disagree, that disagreement is itself the useful signal, and it is why a forecast can say 60% chance of cancelling instead of pretending to know.

Forecasts refresh twice a day, and again whenever you add or change a site.

What you will do next

  2. Add your sites — paste a list or upload a spreadsheet.
  3. Check the limits look right before they start flagging jobs.
